Visual direction: premium 1986 computer magazine cover meets beautifully designed home-computer software packaging. Very dark ink navy background; a small, disciplined CGA-like palette of warm ivory, cyan, dusty magenta and amber. Absolutely no smooth futuristic typography, luminous haze, glossy gradients or rounded modern CTA buttons. Layout: keep generous outer margins and clear hierarchy. Right 57 percent: one large actual app screenshot enclosed by a distinctive ivory and cyan pixel-stepped display frame, subtly evocative of an old computer monitor without bulky photorealistic hardware. Keep the screenshot almost front-on and make both panes and the terminal visible. Sparse pixel lines or a few stepped accent blocks may echo the three-bar logo in the background. Plenty of calm negative space; no decorative icon scatter.

Everything must look deliberately pixel-designed, with a coherent grid and limited palette. Avoid fake CRT blur, heavy scanlines across text, noisy grit, synthwave sunsets, neon cities, wireframe mountains, floating gadgets, fake ratings or extra claims. This is nerdy 8-bit computer nostalgia with excellent graphic design and a clear invitation to try Commander. Deliver one polished edge-to-edge wide banner.
